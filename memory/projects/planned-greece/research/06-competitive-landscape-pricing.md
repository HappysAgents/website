**Finding:** PARTIALLY CONFIRMED but context-dependent

## 3. PRICING MODEL ANALYSIS

### What's Actually Standard in Greece

**The Greek tradesperson market operates on a FLAT RATE PER JOB model, not hourly.**

Evidence:
- Every published price list reviewed prices per job (not per hour)
- The "from €25" visit fee appears to be either: (a) a minimum charge for the visit, or (b) the cost of the cheapest possible job
- No tradesperson in Greece publishes an hourly rate on their website
- Job pricing is highly opaque — final prices negotiated on-site, with materials itemized separately

**Three pricing structures observed:**

1. **Flat rate per job (most common for formal operators):** Set prices per task, materials extra or included depending on operator. This is what Douleutaras and formal service companies use.

2. **No fix = no charge (informal sector):** Tradesperson visits, diagnoses, if repair is simple (e.g., tighten a bolt, reset a switch), may not charge. If actual parts/labour needed, charges €50-80+ flat. This is likely more common with local neighborhood tradespeople.

3. **"From €X" marketing (platform operators):** Lead generation — advertise €25 minimum to get the call, then negotiate/quote on-site. Actual job typically costs €40–150+.

### What This Means for €30/h Assumption

- **Labour COST (to the business): €14/h plumber, €17/h electrician** — these appear broadly correct based on Greek minimum wage (€950/month in 2024 = ~€5.50/h base, but skilled trades ~€1,200–1,500/month gross = ~€7–9/h employer direct cost). Adding social contributions (28%), employer overhead → €10–15/h total cost for a plumber is realistic. **Business plan assumption is BROADLY VALID.**

- **Charge RATE to customer: €30/h is significantly below market.** Market charges €50–90 for a typical job that takes 1–2 hours. If the business charges €30/h, it charges €30–60 for what competitors charge €50–90. This could be positioned as a subscription VALUE (you pay €120/month and get quarterly visits at lower effective rate), but it means the revenue per visit is genuinely low.

- **Recommendation:** Rethink pricing in terms of flat-rate value packages rather than hourly. The business plan should model revenue as "X visits per subscription type × avg. value per visit" rather than "Y hours × €30."

## 7. IMPLICATIONS FOR BUSINESS PLAN

### 7A. Labour Cost Assumptions
**Plan assumed: plumber €14/h, electrician €17/h**

Assessment: **Broadly plausible.** Based on Greek market data:
- Minimum wage (2024): ~€950/month (≈€5.50/h gross)
- Skilled tradespeople likely earn €1,200–1,800/month gross
- At 1,500/month × 12 = €18,000/year gross
- Social contributions (employer) ~28%: adds ~€4,200/year → total employer cost ~€22,200/year
- At 170 working days/year × 8h = 1,360 productive hours: **direct labour cost ≈ €16.3/h**
- Plumber (lower skill) at €1,200/month gross → ~€13.5/h employer cost: **VALIDATES €14/h assumption**
- Electrician (higher skill) at €1,500/month gross → ~€16.8/h employer cost: **VALIDATES €17/h assumption**

### 7B. Charge Rate Assumption
**Plan assumed: charge rate €30/h**

Assessment: **SIGNIFICANTLY LOW vs. market.** Real market charges:
- Typical repair job (1–2 hours): €40–90 flat rate
- That's an effective rate of €25–65/h — but crucially, customers don't think in hourly rates
- A subscription customer paying €120/month gets:
  - Quarterly visit (4×/year = €30/visit amortized)
  - Each visit covers inspection + 1-2 small jobs
  - If inspection finds nothing: labour cost to business ~€15-20 (1h labour), revenue = €30 amortized
  - If inspection fixes small issue: labour cost ~€20-30, revenue = €30 amortized + parts margin
- At €30/h charge rate, a full 3-hour visit generates €90 revenue — this is reasonable
- BUT if competing against market (which charges €50-80 per single job), the subscription must show clear value: multiple preventive checks per quarter that would cost €200-300 if done reactively

**Recommendation:** Keep the unit economics logic but reframe around VALUE not hourly rate. The subscription is priced at €89–149/month (from business plan). Revenue per visit should be modeled as subscription revenue allocated to service delivery, not hourly rate × hours.

### 7C. HomeServe Hellas — Strategic Threat
**CRITICAL UPDATE TO BUSINESS PLAN RISK SECTION**

HomeServe entering Greece in October 2025 changes the competitive picture materially:
- They bring global capital, brand, and operational knowledge
- They are likely targeting the mass market (gas appliances + A/C warranty), not the expat niche
- The business plan's beachhead (foreign property owners) is likely NOT HomeServe's target — they will go after Greek mass market with utility partnerships
- SHORT-TERM: the expat/STR beachhead remains open and uncontested
- MEDIUM-TERM: HomeServe's market education will HELP the category — it legitimizes the concept of paying for home maintenance subscriptions
- LONG-TERM: If HomeServe succeeds and expands scope, they could threaten the general market segment

**Action:** Investigate HomeServe Hellas immediately. If their product is only gas/A/C warranty and not multi-trade preventive inspection, differentiation is achievable.

### 7D. "No Fix = No Charge" Model Impact
If the informal market genuinely operates on no-fix-no-charge, then customers' reference price for a diagnostic visit is €0. Charging anything for a preventive inspection visit may face cultural resistance ("why am I paying €30 for someone to look at things that work fine?").

**Mitigation:**
- Frame subscription as insurance/peace of mind, not per-visit pricing
- Include tangible deliverable per visit: written report, photo documentation, compliance certificate
- Emphasize the TOTAL cost avoidance (reactive repair = €200–500 vs. subscription = €89/month catching it early)

### 7E. Pricing Model Recommendation
Based on market data, revise business plan to:
- Position subscription pricing as significantly below what reactive costs would be (€89-149/month vs. €200-500 for emergency repairs)
- Price individual a-la-carte visits at market rate (€80-120/visit) to make subscription look like strong value
- NOT try to compete with Douleutaras on price for individual jobs — compete on structure, documentation, and English-language service

### Pricing Model Validation

**The Greek market is flat-rate-per-job, not hourly.**
- Visit fee: €25+ (typically includes one small job)
- Small repair job: €20–55
- Medium repair: €40–90
- Drain maintenance subscription (buildings): from €50/month
- Monthly subscription model: EXISTS but only for building drainage, not multi-trade preventive

**"No fix = no charge" model: UNCONFIRMED by formal sources.** All published operators charge for the visit. However, informal tradespeople may waive the visit fee if they find nothing to fix — field research needed to confirm.

### Key Recommendations for Business Plan

1. **Labour cost assumptions (€14/h plumber, €17/h electrician):** VALIDATED ✅
2. **Charge rate (€30/h):** LOW by market standards. Market charges €50-90 for jobs taking 1-2h. Reframe as flat-rate-per-visit model, not hourly.
3. **HomeServe threat:** Immediate competitive research required. If they're focused on gas+A/C warranty (reactive), differentiation is achievable. If they launch preventive inspection subscriptions, this is a serious threat.
4. **B2B channel:** Athens Airbnb management companies (50,000+ listings) are an underserved distribution channel. None have structured preventive maintenance subscriptions. This could be 40-60% of early revenue.
5. **Subscription precedent:** Monthly drainage maintenance (€50+/month/building) already exists in Greece. The subscription maintenance concept is not culturally alien — just not yet applied to multi-trade individual apartment maintenance.
