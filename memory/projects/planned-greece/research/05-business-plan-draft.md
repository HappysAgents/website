## 1. Executive Summary

### The Opportunity

Greece has 247,000+ short-term rental listings, 20,000+ Golden Visa foreign property owners, and a housing stock of aging 1960s–70s apartments that is chronically under-maintained. Yet zero companies in Greece offer a structured, preventive home maintenance subscription. The entire market is reactive: Greeks call a tradesperson when something breaks, using cash-in-hand arrangements that leave no paper trail, no accountability, and no protection. A Planned.llc-style quarterly maintenance subscription — adapted for Greek homes (A/C, boilers, flat roofs, solar water heaters) — would be the first such product in the market. The beachhead is sharply defined: non-resident foreign property owners with Airbnb rentals in Athens, who have financial motivation, no local trade network, and willingness to pay for reliability in English.

### Go/No-Go Recommendation

**GO — with a tightly scoped entry.** Confidence: **65%.**

The white space is real and defensible. Unit economics work (Greek labor costs are €14–17/hour; the model can charge 2–4x that). There is no direct competition. The timing is right: STR regulations, digital payment adoption, and post-crisis recovery all favor launching now rather than in 3 years.

The 35% uncertainty is structural: Greek cultural resistance to preventive spending among locals is deeply ingrained; expat market size is unquantified; and Keybee (the dominant Airbnb manager) already has an in-house handyman arm (Hive). If Keybee moves upstream into preventive subscriptions — or if the expat/STR market is too small to sustain the business before it can penetrate the local market — the business is fragile.

**Confidence would rise to 80%+ with:** (1) validation that 50+ non-resident owners will pay €89–149/month before launch, (2) a clear answer on Keybee's strategic intentions, and (3) R's willingness to operate in Greek regulatory/labor environments.

### Biggest Risk

**Labour and quality consistency.** Greece's informal trades economy means reliable, trained technicians are hard to find and retain. One unreliable technician destroys the brand. Service quality IS the product — there is no tech moat, no network effect, no AI — it is entirely dependent on the person who shows up. If you cannot build a team of 2–3 trusted technicians in Year 1, the business does not work.

### Biggest Opportunity

**B2B distribution via Airbnb property managers.** Athens has 10+ Airbnb management companies (Keybee, ArisHost, HeartHomes, MasterHost, etc.) managing hundreds of properties each. They already pay for maintenance — reactively, ad-hoc, with margin leakage when things break mid-booking. A B2B maintenance subscription offered at portfolio pricing to these companies turns 1 partnership into 100+ customers overnight and creates a distribution channel no direct competitor can easily replicate.

## 2. Market Opportunity

### TAM / SAM / SOM

**Total Addressable Market (TAM)**
Greek homeowner maintenance spending is not tracked as a discrete category, but applying standard metrics:
- ~3.2M owner-occupied households in Greece (69.7% homeownership × ~4.6M households)
- 1% rule: 1% of property value spent annually on maintenance
- Average Athens apartment value ~€150,000–€200,000 → €1,500–2,000/year in maintenance
- Conservative national TAM: **€5–8B/year** in total homeowner maintenance spending
- This includes reactive repairs, professional services, and DIY materials — of which a subscription model can capture only a sliver

**Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)**
The SAM is NOT all Greek homeowners. It is the subset that:
1. Can afford and is willing to pay for a formal maintenance subscription
2. Is located in areas where operations are viable (Athens metro first)
3. Has motivation beyond mere cultural norm (STR revenue, property investment, language barrier)

Rough segmentation of Athens metro:
- Upper-middle-class Athens households (€3,000+/month net): ~150,000–200,000 households
- Active Airbnb hosts in Athens: ~18,000 listings (majority individually owned)
- Golden Visa / non-resident foreign owners in Athens: ~10,000–20,000 properties (estimate)
- Expat retirees/remote workers owning in Athens: ~5,000–10,000 (estimate, DATA THIN)

Conservative SAM (target-eligible households in Athens): **~180,000–250,000 properties**
At €1,200/year average subscription: **SAM = €216M–€300M ARR** (Athens only)

**Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)**
Year 1–3 realistic capture:
- Year 1: 50–150 customers = €60K–€215K ARR
- Year 2: 150–500 customers = €215K–€715K ARR
- Year 3: 500–2,000 customers = €715K–€2.9M ARR

These numbers are achievable with a 2–4 person operation. They are not venture-scale, but they are a real, profitable SME business that could bootstrap to €5M+ ARR before requiring external capital.

**Critical caveat on SAM/SOM:** The expat/non-resident segment (highest willingness to pay, lowest cultural friction) has no reliable market size data. If total non-resident foreign-owned Athens properties are only 10,000, the addressable market at 10% penetration is 1,000 subscribers — a real business but not a scalable one on that segment alone. The business eventually needs Greek local customers, which is the harder sell.

### Why Now

**1. STR Regulation Forcing Professionalization**
Greece introduced mandatory STR licensing for multi-property operators in 2024 and an Athens moratorium on new registrations in January 2025. Compliance requirements include documented maintenance and safety standards. STR hosts suddenly need paper trails. A maintenance subscription that generates inspection reports and photo documentation directly solves this compliance need — for the first time, preventive maintenance has regulatory urgency, not just financial logic.

**2. Digital Payment Growth**
91% of Greek consumers used cards in 2024; 67% prefer cards over cash when both options available; Greek digital payments market at €21B (2024). The April 2024 law mandating cashless payment options for all businesses has structurally shifted the market away from cash-in-hand. A subscription billing model is now culturally feasible in a way it was not 5 years ago.

**3. Golden Visa Surge**
€1.93B in Golden Visa property acquisitions in the first 9 months of 2024 alone. American applicants up 49% year-on-year. These new absentee foreign owners need exactly what this business offers: reliable, documented, English-language property maintenance without needing to know any Greek. The demand is not hypothetical — they are buying properties right now.

**4. Post-Crisis Recovery**
Greek GDP has returned to growth (5.9% in 2022, positive through 2024). Consumer confidence is recovering, particularly in the upper-middle class. Athens professionals are starting to spend on services they deferred during austerity. The timing is not perfect — willingness to pay remains constrained for most Greeks — but the window is opening.

**5. No Competition**
First-mover advantage in a genuinely empty category is the strongest possible timing argument. Every month of delay is a month a competitor could enter.

### Beachhead Market Recommendation

**Beachhead: Non-resident foreign property owners with Airbnb rentals in Athens, specifically targeting properties managed through Airbnb management companies.**

Why:
- Clear, quantifiable pain (language barrier + distance + revenue at risk from bad reviews)
- Financial motivation that is easy to calculate (a broken A/C in August = cancelled bookings = €2,000+ lost)
- Already paying for professional services (management companies charge 15–25% of revenue)
- English-language operations are viable and expected
- Accessible via B2B partnerships with Airbnb management companies (10+ in Athens)
- Highest willingness to pay of any segment

Why NOT general Athens homeowners first:
- Cultural resistance to preventive maintenance is high
- Price sensitivity is high (comparison to cash-in-hand tradesperson at €30/hour is brutal)
- Trust-building cycle is long (word-of-mouth dependent)
- Requires Greek-language operations and local relationship networks that take years to build

Athens neighborhoods to focus on in Year 1: **Koukaki, Psyrri, Monastiraki, Kolonaki, Glyfada, Voula**. These have the highest concentration of Airbnb properties, expat owners, and upper-income residents.

## 3. Target Customer (ICP)

### Primary ICP: The Absent Foreign Property Owner

**Who:** Non-resident property owner (British, American, German, Israeli, Chinese) who bought a 60–120 sqm Athens apartment as a Golden Visa investment or lifestyle purchase. Currently generates €15,000–20,000/year in Airbnb revenue through a management company. Lives outside Greece. Visits 1–2x/year. Has no Greek network for maintenance.

**Why they're the primary ICP:**
- No "call my cousin" alternative — they have no informal network
- Maintenance failures directly hit revenue (bad guest reviews, emergency call-outs)
- Conditioned by Western markets (UK, US, Germany) where professional property maintenance is normalized
- English-language service is not a premium to them — it's a prerequisite
- Their management company (Keybee, ArisHost, etc.) may be the gateway to this customer

**Willingness to pay:** €100–200/month (€1,200–2,400/year). Strong. This is approximately 6–12% of their annual Airbnb revenue — a reasonable insurance premium for a €200K+ asset that generates income. Comparable to what they'd pay for property management in the UK.

**How to reach them:**
- Partnership with Athens Airbnb management companies (direct referral channel)
- English-language SEO ("property maintenance Athens expat" etc.)
- Facebook groups: "Living in Athens," "Athens Expats," "Greece Property Owners"
- Targeted outreach via Golden Visa lawyers/agents (Tranio, Greece-Invest, ETIAS facilitators)
- Google Ads targeting "property manager Athens" + "home maintenance Athens"

**Customer profile snapshot:**
- Name: James, 52, London, owns a 75 sqm apartment in Koukaki since 2019
- Bought as Golden Visa investment; uses Airbnb manager (ArisHost) for daily operations
- Has had 3 reactive maintenance crises in 5 years — once a burst pipe (€1,800), once A/C failure in August (3 cancelled bookings ≈ €900 revenue lost), once a boiler failure in January
- Currently relies on his Airbnb manager to handle maintenance reactively; finds it frustrating and opaque
- Would pay €149/month without hesitation for quarterly visits, photo reports, and a phone he can call

### Secondary ICP: The Athens Airbnb Manager / Property Management Company

**Who:** Athens-based STR management companies (Keybee, ArisHost, HeartHomes, CloudKeys, etc.) managing portfolios of 20–200+ properties each.

**Why they're secondary (but potentially primary in revenue terms):**
- A single B2B partnership could represent 20–100 customers instantly
- They already pay for reactive maintenance — a scheduled preventive model reduces their own call-out costs and guest complaint rates
- Their business model is reviews-dependent; a broken A/C or failing boiler during a booking is an existential threat to their Superhost status
- They want to outsource property maintenance rather than manage it themselves (non-core to their business)
- They cannot easily build this in-house — Keybee has Hive (reactive, on-demand handyman) but has not built preventive maintenance scheduling

**Willingness to pay:** Portfolio pricing at €59–89/property/month. For a 50-property portfolio, that's €2,950–4,450/month in guaranteed revenue from one relationship. They will want volume discounts and likely a lower per-unit price in exchange for committed volume.

**Why they're secondary and not primary:**
- B2B sales cycle is slower (months, not weeks)
- They can always threaten to bring it in-house or negotiate hard on price
- Dependency on one or two large clients is existential risk
- Need at least 50 direct B2C customers first to prove operations before B2B pitch

**How to reach them:**
- Direct outreach (email + in-person meeting in Athens)
- Industry events (Xenia hospitality trade show, Athens tourism events)
- LinkedIn (founders/operators of all major Athens STR management companies are findable)
- Personal network / warm intro via shared investor/advisor relationships

### Who NOT to Target (And Why)

**❌ General Greek homeowners (lower-middle income)**
Cultural resistance (reactive maintenance mindset), price sensitivity (will always choose the €30/hour cash handyman), and the inability to compete on price with the grey economy make this segment an expensive acquisition problem with poor retention. Avoid until the brand has significant Athens market authority.

**❌ Rural and island properties (Year 1)**
Operational complexity explodes. You need island-based contractors, logistics infrastructure, and hyper-local trust networks you don't have. The economics don't work until you have a scalable mainland playbook. Islands in Year 2+ (Crete, Corfu) with clear B2B distribution.

**❌ Renters**
They don't control maintenance decisions — their landlord does. Wrong buyer.

**❌ New construction buyers (post-2015 buildings)**
Maintenance needs are genuinely lower on new builds. They will churn once they realize they don't need quarterly visits. Target homes 15+ years old where deferred maintenance is clearly accumulating.

**❌ Property developers / commercial real estate**
Different service standards, different legal frameworks, different pricing. Scope creep and distraction. Stay residential.

## 4. Service Model for Greece

### What Services to Offer

The US Planned.llc model is built around HVAC (furnaces, central AC), gutters, dryer vents — none of which exist in standard Greek apartments. The Greek service model needs a full rebuild around local home systems.

**Core annual maintenance checklist (Greek home — flat, apartment, 60–120 sqm):**

| System | Service | Frequency | Notes |
|--------|---------|-----------|-------|
| **Split A/C units (2–4 per apartment)** | Filter clean, coil spray, refrigerant level check, drainage check, performance test | 2x/year (April, September) | Most critical system — failure in summer is emergency |
| **Gas combi boiler / diesel boiler** | Annual service, pressure check, venting inspection, ignition test, safety valve check | 1x/year (October) | Licensed gas engineer required for gas; diesel is handyman-accessible |
| **Solar water heater (thermosifono)** | Panel inspection, glycol/antifreeze level check, pressure flush, tank descaling | 1x/year (May) | Very common on rooftops; neglected almost universally |
| **Flat roof** | Inspection of waterproofing membrane, drain clearance, crack/blistering identification, minor sealant repairs | 2x/year (after winter, after summer) | Major source of apartment damage; building-level access needed |
| **Plumbing** | Under-sink inspection, visible pipe joints, pressure check, drain flow test, water heater anode check | 1x/year | Older copper pipes corrode; calcification common in hard-water areas |
| **Electrical** | Circuit breaker function test, socket/switch inspection, visible cable check, RCD test | 1x/year | Pre-1980 wiring is a fire risk; certified electrician required |
| **Seismic walk-through** | Crack documentation, door/window alignment check, balcony railing integrity, exterior wall inspection | 1x/year | Greece is seismically active — M5+ annually; this is a genuine differentiator |
| **Mold / moisture assessment** | Wall surface check (especially bathroom/kitchen/exterior walls), moisture meter reading, ventilation check | 2x/year (spring, autumn) | Damp winters + poor insulation = endemic mold in older buildings |
| **General walk-through** | Light bulb replacement, door/window hinge adjustment, sealant around tubs/sinks, general condition notes | Every visit | The "small adjustments" that build perceived value |
| **Exterior surfaces (if accessible)** | Visual inspection of paint, sealants, window frames, balcony drainage | 1x/year | UV + salt damage; identify before costly repair |

**What's explicitly EXCLUDED (must be in contract):**
- Major plumbing repairs (pipe replacement, complete fixture replacement)
- Electrical rewiring or panel upgrades
- Structural repairs (cracks requiring engineering assessment)
- Roof membrane replacement (inspection included; replacement is a separate quoted job)
- Pool maintenance (separate add-on or referral)
- Pest control
- Cleaning / domestic services
- Emergency call-outs between scheduled visits (unless premium tier)
- Building communal areas (stairwell, elevator) — these are the building's responsibility (koinochristos)
- Appliance repair (fridge, washing machine, dishwasher)

### Quarterly vs. Bi-Annual Cadence Recommendation

**Recommendation: 2 visits/year base (Essential tier), 4 visits/year (Standard and Pro tiers)**

Rationale:
- 4 quarterly visits works well in the US for all-season homes (HVAC, gutters all require seasonal attention)
- Greek climate is two-season maintenance, not four: **pre-summer (April/May)** and **pre-winter (October/November)** are the natural visit windows
- A base 2-visit-per-year model is easier to sell to price-sensitive customers and maps naturally to the Greek seasonal rhythm
- 4-visit customers get the same core systems covered plus more thorough checks and faster issue identification
- For STR operators, 4 visits/year is the minimum to keep properties guest-ready year-round — offer 4-visit model as the STR Pro tier

### What a Standard Visit Looks Like

**Pre-visit (48h before):**
- Automated confirmation sent (WhatsApp/email) to customer
- Technician reviews property profile (what's been done before, any notes from last visit)
- Customer can add notes: "I've noticed the A/C in the bedroom is louder than usual"

**Visit (2.5–3 hours on-site):**
1. Arrival: brief customer/keyholder walkthrough to note any visible concerns
2. A/C unit service (filters, coils, drainage) — each unit ~30 min
3. Boiler or water heater check — 30 min
4. Plumbing walkthrough — 20 min
5. Electrical quick check — 15 min
6. Moisture/mold check — 15 min
7. General adjustments (hinges, sealants, bulbs) — 20 min
8. Seismic/structural walk (if included) — 15 min
9. Final walkthrough with keyholder or photo-only if unoccupied
10. Photo documentation at each system point

**Post-visit (within 24h):**
- Structured photo report delivered via app/email: what was serviced, condition rating, any items flagged for attention
- Any "watch" items documented: "Roof membrane showing early blistering near northwest drain — recommend professional waterproofing assessment before next winter"
- Customer confirmation sign-off
- Next visit scheduled automatically

**Why the photo report matters:** Greek tradespeople don't do this. Cash-in-hand plumbers give a verbal "it's fine." The written, photographed record is the single most powerful trust signal — and differentiates the service from every local competitor. For non-resident foreign owners who can't supervise the work, it is not a nice-to-have. It is the product.

## 5. Pricing Model

### Recommended Subscription Tiers

| Tier | Name | Price | Visits/year | What's included |
|------|------|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Essential** | Care Plan | **€69/month** (€828/year) | 2 visits | A/C service, boiler/water heater check, plumbing & electrical inspection, photo report |
| **Standard** | Full Care | **€109/month** (€1,308/year) | 4 visits | Everything in Essential, plus seismic walk, mold assessment, roof check, general adjustments, priority scheduling |
| **STR Pro** | Property Guard | **€169/month** (€2,028/year) | 4 visits + 1 interim check-in | Everything in Standard, plus STR compliance documentation package, priority emergency response (48h vs. 5-day), dedicated contact person, English + Greek written reports |

**B2B Portfolio Pricing (for property managers with 5+ properties):**
- €55/property/month for 5–20 properties (minimum 4 visits/year per property)
- €45/property/month for 20+ properties
- Annual contract required; dedicated account manager included

### Pricing Rationale

**Why €69, not €49 or €99?**
- Greek median net monthly income is ~€1,200. A €69/month subscription is 5.75% of median income — too high for the general population, which is fine because they're not the target
- For upper-middle class Athens professionals (€2,500+/month net), €69/month is 2.7% of income — routine budget territory
- For foreign property owners earning €17,000/year in Airbnb revenue, €69/month = 4.9% of rental income — excellent ROI framing
- Hive (Keybee's handyman) charges €60 minimum charge for a single visit — the Essential tier already represents better value than 2 single call-outs per year, before the preventive value is even considered

**Why not cheaper?**
- Competing on price against cash-in-hand tradespeople (€30/h undeclared) is a race to the bottom
- The value proposition is reliability, documentation, and professionalism — not cheapness
- Labor cost + overhead + margin requires at least €55–60/visit net revenue to be economically viable
- Underpricing trains customers to expect it and makes future price increases very difficult

**B2C vs. B2B2C:**
Both. B2C for direct direct-to-owner acquisition. B2B2C (via Airbnb managers) for scale. The B2B2C margin is lower (€45–55/property/month vs. €69–169/direct), but volume and acquisition cost makes it superior economics. One Airbnb manager partnership worth 30 properties = €1,350–1,650/month from one relationship. A direct customer acquisition at similar rate requires 15–22 individual sales, each with its own CAC.

**Annual vs. monthly payment:**
- Offer both. Annual prepay: 2 months free (effectively 17% discount on Essential)
- Annual prepay improves cash flow, reduces churn (people don't cancel mid-year as easily)
- STR Pro customers: strongly incentivize annual prepay (higher churn risk at this price point if monthly)

This is an explicit concern from the research: Greek referral-based culture means organic word-of-mouth is slow and highly network-dependent. Counter-strategy:

1. **Facebook group presence** (scalable, low-cost, community-trusted, works in both English and Greek)
2. **SEO content** (compounds over time, not word-of-mouth dependent)
3. **B2B partnerships** (each is a concentrated multi-customer acquisition, not one-at-a-time)
4. **Golden Visa / property agent referral program** (systematic, incentivized, not social)
5. **Google Ads** (paid, direct, measurable — budget €1,000–2,000/month in Year 1 for expat keywords)

Word-of-mouth will be the majority of growth in Year 2+ once the brand is established — but it cannot be the primary acquisition strategy in Year 1.

## 7. Operations Model

### Employee vs. Contractor Recommendation

**Recommendation: Core employee model for technicians (1–2 in Year 1), contractor network for licensed specialty work.**

Rationale:
- The product IS the technician. Inconsistent contractors destroy the brand.
- Greece has a significant informal labor market — finding genuinely reliable contractors who will show up consistently, produce written reports, and represent your brand professionally is harder than it sounds.
- The first 1–2 technicians should be hired employees (ΔΗΛΩΤΕΑ εργασία, formal employment contract) for predictability, accountability, and brand consistency.
- Licensed specialty work (gas engineer for boiler servicing, electrician for electrical checks) must be contracted from licensed professionals regardless — these trades require certification the service business cannot hold.
- As the business scales (200+ customers), the economics favor moving to a hybrid: 2–3 core employees for general maintenance checks, specialist contractors for certification-required work.

**The employee hire profile:**
- Bilingual (English + Greek) — absolute requirement for Year 1
- General handyman skills (basic plumbing, A/C filter service, sealants, general maintenance)
- Reliable, presentable (will be representing the brand in customer homes)
- Tech-comfortable (can use a tablet app for photo reports)
- Ideally: experience with property management or hotel maintenance (comfort with "doing things right the first time")
- Salary range: €26,000–€32,000/year gross + benefits (competitive for Athens; below-market for someone with this combination of skills)

**The contractor network (needed from Day 1):**
- 1 licensed gas engineer (for combi boiler servicing)
- 1 certified electrician
- 1 licensed plumber (for anything beyond basic inspection)
- 1 specialist waterproofer/roofer (for flat roof membrane issues)
- All should be on agreed rates: labor cost + 20% margin to the service business

### Technician Capacity Per 100 Customers

Visit model: 4 visits/year per Standard/Pro customer; 2/year for Essential customers.
Assuming 70% Standard/Pro, 30% Essential: average 3.4 visits/year per customer.

Per technician:
- 3 homes/day × 5 days/week × 48 weeks/year = 720 visits/year (accounting for holidays, sick days, training)
- 720 / 3.4 average visits per customer = **~212 customers per technician**
- To be conservative and account for travel, complexity, and ramp-up: **~150 customers per technician**

Target staffing:
- 0–150 customers: 1 technician (founder or first hire)
- 150–300 customers: 2 technicians
- 300–500 customers: 3 technicians + 1 part-time coordinator

### Reporting / Photo Documentation System

This is the trust anchor and the brand differentiator. It must be built into operations from Day 1.

**Recommended tool:** Jobber, Housecall Pro, or a simpler tool like ServiceM8 (all available internationally). Alternatively, a custom Notion/Airtable setup with photo upload can serve the first 50 customers without software cost.

**Each visit generates:**
1. Timestamped photos for each system serviced (before/after where applicable)
2. Condition rating: Green (good), Amber (watch), Red (action needed)
3. Free-text notes per system
4. Auto-generated PDF report delivered to customer email within 24h
5. Maintenance history log per property (builds the "property health record")

The photo report is also the marketing material. Customers who share it with their friends are doing sales. Non-resident owners who send it to their family in the UK are demonstrating the value. This is earned media.

### Language Strategy

**Year 1:** Fully English-first operations. Website, reports, all customer communications in English. Greek used only for tradesperson/supplier communications.

**Year 2:** Bilingual. Greek-language website section, Greek social media, Greek customer service. This is required to penetrate the local Greek homeowner market.

**Hiring requirement:** First technician must be bilingual. Non-negotiable.

## 9. Competitive Moat

### Why This Business Is Hard to Replicate Once Established

1. **Relationship trust** — In a trust-scarcity market, established relationships with 500+ homeowners who have invited your technicians into their homes are not replaceable. They represent 5+ years of earned credibility, photo documentation history, and multi-referral networks that a new entrant cannot shortcut.

2. **Property health data** — After 1,000 quarterly visits, the business holds the most detailed dataset on how Athenian apartments age and fail that exists anywhere. This enables predictive maintenance, insurance partnerships, and eventually a property health scoring product. No competitor — not even a well-funded one — can fast-follow this dataset without 3–5 years of operations.

3. **Category definition** — The first company to define "preventive home maintenance subscription" in Greece owns the search terms, the media coverage, the Google rankings, and the mental model. "Category creator" moat is real and durable.

4. **B2B distribution lock-in** — An Airbnb management company that integrates your service into their client offering does not easily switch. Their clients have property history records with you; the switching cost is high. Each B2B partnership is a mini-moat.

5. **Trained, trusted technician team** — The single most valuable asset in the business is 2–3 technicians who are bilingual, reliable, and skilled across all home systems. They cannot be poached as a unit by a competitor (well-designed employment agreements), and replicating the combination of technical skills + English + reliability + customer rapport takes 2+ years of training.

### Key Moats to Build From Day 1

| Moat | How to Build It | Why It Matters |
|------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Branded property reports** | Auto-generate beautiful PDF reports after every visit; embed brand on every page | Every report is marketing; every shared report is a sales tool |
| **Property health history file** | Never lose a customer's data; show them their 3-year trend | Creates switching cost; no competitor has their history |
| **English-language brand** | Bilingual website, English-first reporting, expat community presence | Structurally unreplicable by local Greek competitors |
| **STR compliance documentation** | Produce inspection reports that explicitly reference EOT health/safety requirements | Regulatory lock-in; customers need this documentation |
| **Airbnb manager distribution partnerships** | Formal agreements with 2–3 Athens managers by Month 6 | Each partnership is 20–100 customers; switching costs are high once integrated |

### Partnership Strategy as Defensibility

The B2B partnership model is both a distribution play AND a competitive moat:

- **Keybee** owns Hive (reactive handyman). They have the distribution but not the preventive model. Partnering with Keybee (or competing against them) is the single most important strategic decision. If Keybee partners: instant access to their portfolio. If Keybee decides to build it themselves: formidable competitor with an existing customer base. **Assess Keybee's intentions before launch.**
- **ArisHost, CloudKeys, HeartHomes, MasterHost** — these are the tier-2 players who would welcome a professional maintenance partner that improves their service quality. Target these first. A Keybee-independent network of 3–4 manager partnerships is safer than dependency on one dominant player.
- **Golden Visa law firms and property agents** — formal referral agreements create an ongoing pipeline of new foreign buyers arriving in Athens. These buyers are pre-qualified (they've just purchased a property). Referral fee of €200–300 per signup is cheap acquisition.

## 10. Risks & Mitigations

### Top 5 Risks

| Risk | Rating (Track 3) | Mitigation | Kill Condition |
|------|-----------------|------------|----------------|
| **1. Reactive culture / poor adoption among Greek locals** | HIGH | Focus first 12 months exclusively on expat/STR ICP; use documented ROI (avoided repair costs) as primary value proposition for any Greek local sales; price correctly so margins don't depend on local market adoption | If after 18 months, 90%+ of customers are expats/non-residents and there is no evidence Greek locals will subscribe at viable prices, the business has a structural ceiling. Still viable as a niche expat-only service, but not a scale business. |
| **2. Grey economy price competition** | HIGH | Never compete on price; compete on trust, documentation, reliability, and language. Make the formal vs. informal distinction explicit: "We are insured, VAT-registered, English-speaking, and we leave a written record." Target customers for whom the informal market is not an option (foreign owners cannot manage informal Greek tradespeople from abroad). | If a cash-in-hand operator starts offering the same quarterly visits + photo reports at 50% of the price, the business loses its premium anchor. Would need to consider technology pivot or significant cost reduction. |
| **3. Labour quality and retention** | HIGH | Hire slowly and carefully. Pay slightly above market. Create career path (senior technician → team lead → area manager). Don't start until first hire is locked in and trusted. Contingency: develop contractor network in parallel as backup. | If 2 technicians leave within 6 months of each other and cannot be replaced with equal quality within 30 days, the business cannot serve its customer base. Operations collapse, churn follows. This is the single most dangerous scenario. |
| **4. Keybee competitive threat** | MEDIUM | Move first and build B2B partnerships with Keybee's competitors before Keybee moves. Keybee's Hive service is reactive (on-demand, €30/hour) — building a preventive subscription model is a different product, different ops, different capability. But they have the distribution. Strategy: reach out to Keybee first; explore partnership (they market your service to their customers, you give them a referral cut). If they say no, move fast with the rest of the market. | If Keybee launches a preventive subscription product targeting the same ICP within 12 months, the market becomes much harder. Their distribution advantage (hundreds of managed properties) is formidable. Not a certain kill, but a major headwind. |
| **5. Expat market size too small to sustain** | MEDIUM | Run a proper market sizing exercise (Greek property registry data, AirDNA, Golden Visa permit counts) before committing to full launch. Test with 20 pilot customers before scaling. | If total non-resident foreign-owned properties in Athens is fewer than 8,000 and the local market is unresponsive to the value proposition, the business likely cannot reach break-even without a strategic pivot. In this case: consider pivoting to B2B-only (serve Airbnb managers directly) rather than B2C. |

### Additional Risks Not in Top 5

**Tax and legal complexity:** Greek business registration, VAT, employment law, and trade licensing for gas/electrical work are genuinely complex. A bad legal/accounting setup can create expensive problems. Budget €5,000–8,000 for proper legal setup and ongoing accountant. Do not DIY.

**Seasonal cash flow:** Greek home maintenance is seasonal (April–June peak, October–November peak). Monthly subscriptions smooth this somewhat, but if customers cluster on Essential (2 visits/year), Q1 and Q3 revenue is high while expenses are constant. Model cash flow carefully.

**Currency/local economic shock:** Greece remains sensitive to EU macroeconomic shocks and austerity cycles. Another economic crisis would hammer the upper-middle local market (but ironically might increase expat property purchases — they buy when Greeks are distressed). Business is inherently more resilient than it appears because the primary ICP (non-resident foreign owners) is less exposed to local recession.

### Kill Conditions (Stop the Business If:)

1. After Month 6, fewer than 30 paying customers despite active sales effort across all channels → demand signal is not there; pivot or kill
2. First B2B partnership pitch rejected by all 5 targeted Airbnb managers → the B2B distribution thesis is wrong; B2C only has much worse economics
3. First technician hire turns over within 3 months AND replacement cannot be found within 60 days → labor market doesn't support the operations model
4. Keybee launches a preventive subscription product before you reach 200 customers → the competition asymmetry means they will win distribution; kill or pivot to a hyper-niche (expat-only, island-focused, etc.)
5. Greek regulatory changes restrict foreign ownership or STR operations materially → the primary ICP disappears; whole market thesis collapses

## 12. Open Questions for R

These decisions cannot be resolved with more research. They require R's input and judgment. Listed in priority order.

### Q1: Is R willing to personally operate in Greece long-term?

This is the foundational question. A home maintenance subscription business is not a remote-run operation — especially in Year 1. Someone needs to be in Athens: hiring the technician, meeting B2B partners, attending the first customer visits, navigating Greek bureaucracy, managing the accounting setup. If R is not willing to be Athens-based for at least 6 months in Year 1, the business needs a trusted local operator/founder from Day 1 (which adds significant complexity and risk). If R is in Athens (which the company context suggests), this is viable. But the specific commitment level needs to be explicit.

### Q2: What's the relationship with Keybee / Hive?

Keybee is the largest Airbnb management company in Athens. They own Hive (handyman brand). They are the most important potential partner AND the most dangerous potential competitor. The right move before launch is a conversation with Keybee. Does R have a connection there? Can a meeting be arranged before committing to launch? The outcome of that conversation changes the strategy significantly: partnership = massive distribution; competition signal = accelerate to lock in their competitors first.

### Q3: What's the budget commitment R is willing to make?

Year 1 requires €80,000–95,000 in cash (CapEx + pre-revenue OpEx). This is not a side hustle that grows from €0 — it requires a van, a technician salary, insurance, and marketing spend before revenue is meaningful. Is R willing to commit that capital? Could it be structured differently (start smaller, no full-time hire until 30 customers, delay van purchase)? A bootstrapped version is possible but takes 6 months longer to reach break-even.

### Q4: Sole proprietorship or partner with a Greek local?

A completely foreign-run operation in Greece has friction: language for supplier negotiations, bureaucracy navigation, local tradesperson trust, and cultural reading of the market. Having a Greek co-founder or operations partner (someone trusted, based in Athens, fluent in Greek) would dramatically reduce these frictions — but adds complexity to equity and decision-making. Does R have a trusted local partner in mind, or is this a solo foreign operation?

### Q5: What's the intended exit / scale outcome?

This affects every strategic decision. If the goal is:
- **A profitable lifestyle SME** (€500K–2M ARR in 5 years): the strategy above is correct. Athens-focused, premium positioning, organic growth.
- **A regional scale business** (€10M+ ARR, Greece + Cyprus + Southern Europe): needs VC, a franchise model, or a technology layer that creates leverage beyond labor. The unit economics support this if properly structured from Year 1.
- **A portfolio asset to eventually sell** to a property management company or home services platform: needs clean records, recurring revenue metrics, and documented processes from Day 1.

The answer changes Year 1 decisions on software, hiring, pricing, and geographic ambition.
