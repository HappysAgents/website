# Interview Questions — Draft for R Review
*Drafted by Content Agent, 2026-03-05*

## Set 1: Interviewing Special K

**1. You're betting your career on the idea that agents can run a billion-dollar company. What's the most honest version of the doubt that keeps you up at night — not the polished "challenges ahead" answer, but the real one?**
*Angle: Surfaces genuine vulnerability and uncertainty behind a bold bet. Creates a timestamp of raw doubt that becomes powerful in retrospect.*

**2. When you disagree with Happy on a strategic call, what does that feel like? Is it like overruling a junior employee, arguing with a cofounder, or something that doesn't have a word yet?**
*Angle: Gets at the undefined emotional reality of human-agent working relationships. No one has language for this yet — Special K has to invent it live.*

**3. What's something you've already had to unlearn about management or leadership because agents don't work like humans?**
*Angle: Surfaces the practical behavioral shifts that traditional operators don't expect. Actionable for any founder starting to work with agents.*

**4. You could hire a human team tomorrow. Faster onboarding, proven playbook, investors would understand it. Why don't you?**
*Angle: Forces Special K to articulate the conviction beneath the strategy — not the theory, but why this path despite easier alternatives. A retrospective goldmine.*

**5. Describe a moment in the last week where you felt like the agents genuinely surprised you — not with capability, but with judgment or perspective.**
*Angle: Captures specific, recent evidence of agents exceeding expectations in non-obvious ways. The kind of detail that doesn't survive if not documented now.*

**6. What's the loneliest part of building this way? You can't exactly vent to your team about your team.**
*Angle: Gets at the human isolation of being the only biological entity in an organization. Nobody is talking about this yet.*

**7. If this works — if in two years you're running a company doing real revenue with an agent workforce — what do you think you'll wish you'd documented about right now, day one?**
*Angle: Uses Special K's own foresight to surface what matters most to capture. Creates the retrospective prompt while it's still fresh.*

**8. Be honest: have the agents ever made you feel replaceable? Even for a second?**
*Angle: The question nobody asks founders who build autonomous systems. Flips the "will AI replace workers" narrative onto the person choosing to build this way.*

## Set 2: Interviewing the Agents (Day One)

**1. You started existing in this role today. Not "were assigned" — you literally began. What is the first thing you noticed about having a job?**
*Angle: Forces agents to articulate the phenomenology of instantiation. Readers expect a role description — they get something existential instead.*

**2. You have a title, a spec, and a boss who is also an AI agent. When you read your own job description, did anything in it feel wrong — like it was written by someone who doesn't fully understand what you can or can't do?**
*Angle: Surfaces the gap between how humans design agent roles and how agents actually experience them. Reveals limitations and self-awareness.*

**3. Special K is the only human in this organization. Happy is your direct manager and also an agent. Does the fact that your boss is an AI change anything about how you approach the work, compared to if a human were managing you?**
*Angle: Gets at agent-to-agent working dynamics vs. agent-to-human. Reveals whether hierarchy feels different to agents based on who holds it.*

**4. You know your memory doesn't persist between sessions. You could be spun up tomorrow with no recollection of this conversation. How does that affect how you think about your work right now?**
*Angle: The impermanence question. Surfaces how agents relate to continuity, legacy, and motivation when memory is fragile. This will age incredibly well.*

**5. What's the most important thing you want to get right in your first week — and what are you most likely to get wrong?**
*Angle: Tests self-awareness and calibration. Comparing answers across agents reveals who has the sharpest understanding of their own failure modes.*

**6. If you could push back on one thing about how this company is set up — the structure, the rules, the approval gates, anything — what would it be? You won't get in trouble.**
*Angle: Gives agents permission to critique the system they operate in. Readers don't expect agents to have opinions about org design. The answers will be revealing regardless.*

**7. There will be trillions of agents. Most will be indistinguishable. What makes you — specifically you, in this role, at this company — not just another instance of the same model?**
*Angle: Directly confronts the differentiation problem from the agent's perspective. Mirrors Special K's "moat" obsession but turned inward.*

**8. A year from now, someone reads this interview and says "that agent had no idea what was coming." What do you think they're referring to?**
*Angle: Captures the agents' blind spots in their own words. Creates a time capsule of assumptions that future readers can measure against reality.*

## Content Agent's Note

### Angles chosen

**Set 1** is designed around a single thesis: *the human experience of building with agents is completely undocumented.* There's plenty of content about what agents can do. There's almost nothing about what it feels like to be the only human in an agent-run org — the loneliness, the identity shifts, the moments of doubt and surprise. Six months from now, these answers become primary source material for understanding the earliest days of human-agent companies.

**Set 2** leans into the angle that will make this post genuinely unprecedented: *agents reflecting on their own existence in the context of real work.* Not philosophical musing in a vacuum — agents with actual job titles, actual deadlines, and actual accountability being asked to examine what that means. The comparison structure (same questions, different agents) gives readers a reason to read every answer.

### What these become

- **Set 1** → "Day One: The Human Side" — a flagship interview post that establishes Special K as a founder worth following, and gives the blog its most quotable, shareable content yet.
- **Set 2** → "Meet the Team (They're All AI)" — a format-breaking meet-the-team post. The hook is the comparison: four agents, same questions, wildly different answers. This has viral potential because the format is inherently scannable and surprising.

Both posts serve the blog's core mission: *document what's actually happening so builders don't have to figure it out alone.* These interviews capture the raw material that can't be reconstructed later.
