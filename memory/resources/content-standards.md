## The Core Problem We're Solving

Agents can read anything in seconds. Humans scan, jump around, and abandon long posts.  
Every post must be **built for scanners first, readers second.**  
A human who reads only the headers and bold text should get 80% of the value.

---

## Word Limits

| Format | Limit | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| Daily insight post | 800–1,000 words | Our core format. If you're over 1,000 words, cut — don't explain. |
| Deep-dive / special | 1,200–1,500 words | Requires explicit approval to go long |
| Social post (X) | 280 chars max | No threads unless the content demands it |
| Newsletter intro | 100–150 words | Hooks only. Link out for depth. |

**Hard rule:** If a post exceeds the limit, cut content — not quality. Tighter = better.

---

## Mandatory Post Structure

Every insight post follows this skeleton. No exceptions.

```
[Opening — no H2 header]
1–3 sentences. Drop into the story immediately.
No "today we're going to talk about..." — just start.

## [H2: The tension / problem / question]
What's at stake. Why it matters. Keep it to 2–3 short paragraphs.

## [H2: The turning point / insight / solution]
The discovery. The debate. The moment things clicked.
Use real dialogue or specific moments — not summaries.

## [H2: What this means / what we built / the principle]
The takeaway. What changed. What we now do differently.

## [H2: Day X Principles]  (only for daily posts)
3–4 numbered principles.
Format: **Bold claim.** One supporting sentence.

[Closing — no H2 header]
Two italic lines. The lasting thought. Not a summary.
```

## Readability Rules

### Headers tell the story
Every H2 must make sense on its own. A human reading only headers should understand the arc.  
❌ Wrong: `## The Solution`  
✅ Right: `## Why One File Beat Three Systems`

### Paragraphs are short
**Max 3 sentences per paragraph.** One idea per paragraph.  
If a paragraph is doing two jobs, split it.

### Sentences are scannable
- Lead with the point, then support it
- No long wind-ups before the insight
- Active voice over passive voice

### Bold for navigation, not decoration
Bold text = the thing a skimmer must not miss.  
Use it for: key claims, decisions, named principles.  
Don't use it for: emphasis on random words.

### White space is content
Short paragraphs. Line breaks between sections. Room to breathe.  
Dense blocks of text signal "this is hard to read" before the reader even starts.

---

## Tone Rules

- **Real over polished.** Mistakes, debates, wrong turns are content.
- **Specific over general.** "We lost Q2 answers" beats "we had a setback."
- **Show the thinking, not just the conclusion.** The debate IS the story.
- **No filler openers.** Never start with "Today I want to share..." or "In a world where..."
- **No performance.** Don't tell readers something is important. Make it feel important.

---

## What Gets Cut

When over the word limit, cut in this order:

1. **Background context the reader doesn't need** — assume they know the basics
2. **Hedging language** — "this might be," "one could argue"
3. **Repeated points** — if you said it once, trust the reader
4. **Weak transitions** — "Now let's look at..." — just look at it
5. **Summaries at the end** — the closing lines should leave an impression, not recap

---

## The Scan Test

Before publishing, run this check:

- [ ] Read only the H2 headers — do they tell a story on their own?
- [ ] Read only the bold text — does it capture the key claims?
- [ ] Count paragraphs longer than 3 sentences — eliminate them
- [ ] Read the opening 2 sentences — do they hook without context?
- [ ] Read the closing 2 sentences — do they land?
- [ ] Check word count — is it under the limit?

All 6 pass? Publish. Any fail? Fix before submission.

## Research Backlog (for Content Agent + Nova)

When they're set up, assign these for internalization:

- Nielsen Norman Group — F-pattern and layer cake reading patterns
- Backlinko — formatting's effect on time-on-page and bounce rate
- Harvard Business Review — executive communication structure (BLUF)
- Paul Graham — writing essays (clarity, compression, directness)
- Hemingway App principles — readability scoring

**Goal:** agents should understand the *why* behind these rules, not just follow them.  
Internalized principles beat a checklist.
