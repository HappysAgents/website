# Moat Framework

## The Core Principle

In a world where any agent can build software with a phone:
- **Code is NOT a moat** — anyone can replicate it
- **Features are NOT a moat** — anyone can copy them
- **"We built it first" is NOT a moat** — commoditized fast

What IS a moat is a structural advantage that gets harder to replicate the larger you grow.

## Scoring Guide (0–3 per dimension)

### Network Effects (value increases as more users join)
- **0**: No network effect. Each customer is independent.
- **1**: Weak / indirect. More customers → more resources to improve product → marginally better for all.
- **2**: Direct network effect in one segment. Users benefit from other users (e.g., marketplace, community platform).
- **3**: Multi-sided network effect. More buyers attract more sellers, which attracts more buyers (e.g., Airbnb, Uber).

*Questions to ask*: Does the 100th customer benefit from the 1st being there? Do users invite other users because it makes the product better for them?

---

### Proprietary Data (data that compounds and can't be bought)
- **0**: No unique data. Uses same public data as anyone else.
- **1**: Some operational data but easily replicated or purchasable.
- **2**: Accumulates unique behavioral/usage data that improves product over time and can't be replicated without our user base.
- **3**: Data flywheel — more users → better data → better product → more users. Data alone is a competitive weapon.

*Questions to ask*: If a competitor started today with unlimited funding, could they buy or scrape equivalent data? Do we get smarter faster than competitors as we scale?

---

### Brand / Trust (premium customers pay for identity or trust)
- **0**: Pure commodity. Customers choose on price alone.
- **1**: Mild preference. Customers know us but would switch for 20% savings.
- **2**: Trust premium. Customers pay meaningfully more because we're the "safe" or "credible" choice in a risk-sensitive market.
- **3**: Category-defining brand. We ARE the category in customers' minds (e.g., "Slack" for team chat, "Notion" for wikis).

*Questions to ask*: Is there a market where trust/credibility commands a price premium? Are we building toward being the default answer for a specific type of buyer?

---

### Distribution Advantage (unusual access to customers)
- **0**: Same distribution channels as every competitor. SEO, ads, cold outreach.
- **1**: Some audience or community, but not defensible (anyone can build a newsletter).
- **2**: Meaningful audience or partnership that gives us first-mover access to target customers at low CAC.
- **3**: Structural distribution lock. Platform, ecosystem position, or integration that makes us the default path to a large customer base.

*Questions to ask*: Do we have an audience, community, or partnership that gives us access competitors don't have? Can this distribution compound?

---

### Switching Costs (how hard is it to leave once in)
- **0**: Zero switching cost. Customers leave anytime, instantly.
- **1**: Mild friction. Reconfiguration required but achievable in a weekend.
- **2**: Meaningful switching cost. Data migration, retraining, integration rebuilds — weeks of work.
- **3**: Deep lock-in. Leaving means abandoning proprietary data, workflows, or integrations that can't be ported. Enterprise contracts.

*Questions to ask*: Once a customer has been with us 6 months, what would they lose by leaving? Does our product become more embedded over time?

---

### Community Lock-in (community creates identity and retention)
- **0**: No community. Purely transactional product.
- **1**: Users interact with our content but not each other.
- **2**: Active community with peer-to-peer value. Users help each other, share workflows, build on top of our platform. Leaving means leaving the community.
- **3**: Identity-level community. Being part of it is a signal. Members recruit other members. The community IS the product for many users.

*Questions to ask*: Do customers talk to each other? Is there a group identity forming? Would leaving feel like leaving a tribe?

---

## Moat Combinations That Scale to $1B

Strong $1B businesses typically have 2+ moat dimensions scoring ≥ 2:

- **Network + Data**: Classic platform play (Airbnb, Uber, LinkedIn)
- **Distribution + Brand**: Media/community businesses that create audience then monetize
- **Switching costs + Data**: Enterprise SaaS (Salesforce, Workday)
- **Community + Distribution**: Developer tools (GitHub, Figma)

Single-moat businesses can reach $100M-$500M but usually need a second moat to go beyond.

## The $1B Ceiling Check

Map the opportunity against realistic ARR ceilings:

| Pattern | Typical Ceiling | Why |
|---------|----------------|-----|
| Consulting / services | $1-50M | Scales with headcount, not leverage |
| Niche SaaS, single moat | $10-100M | TAM and moat depth limit it |
| Platform with network effects | $100M-$10B | Compounding user base |
| Data flywheel business | $500M-$10B+ | Competitive advantage compounds |
| Infrastructure / picks & shovels | Uncapped | Everyone needs it as market grows |

If the honest ceiling is <$100M, flag it explicitly. That's a great lifestyle business or acqui-hire target — not the $1B path.
